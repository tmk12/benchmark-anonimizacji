IN THE COUNTY COURT AT SOUTHAMPTON
Claim No. H0ST2506
BETWEEN Diane Barnes (Claimant) and Donna Palmer (Defendant)
JUDGMENT
25 March 2024
Before: District Judge Hernandez, sitting alone.
Counsel for the Claimant: Barbara Adams. Counsel for the Defendant: Gary Sanchez.
Heard on 11 March 2024. Judgment handed down on 25 March 2024. This judgment was given after trial of a claim for the price of goods and services supplied under a written contract.
1. This is a claim by Diane Barnes for the unpaid price of works carried out under a written agreement. Donna Palmer defends the claim on the grounds of part performance and set-off.
2. By a claim form issued on 31 July 2023 the Claimant seeks £4,066.34 together with interest from 3 July 2023. The Defendant filed a defence resisting the claim in full.
3. On 8 February 2023 the parties entered into a written contract under which the claimant undertook to carry out certain works and the defendant undertook to pay the price within fourteen days of acceptance. The works were accepted without reservation.
4. The price was not paid by 3 July 2023. A letter requiring payment was sent on 11 July 2023. It went unanswered.
5. Mary Wilson gave evidence for the claimant and Kathleen Cook gave evidence for the defendant. I found both witnesses honest, although their recollections differed in detail.
6. The expert evidence of Melissa Ramirez, a quantity surveyor, was agreed in all material respects and the court accepts it as reliable and persuasive.
7. The Claimant's right to payment arises from the contract and from section 49 of the Sale of Goods Act 1979 as regards the price of goods. The burden of proving payment or a valid set-off lay on the Defendant.
8. The principle that a party who accepts work without reservation cannot later resist payment on the basis of defects readily apparent on inspection is stated in Johnson v Fisher [2021] EWCA Civ 1037, where the court held that acceptance is strong evidence of conformity with the contract.
9. See also Bush v Gill [2022] 1 WLR 261, in which it was decided that interest runs from the contractual due date without any further demand.
10. Compare Lawrence v Stewart [2023] AC 1175, and the authorities cited there on the assessment of damages for unpaid contract sums.
11. The defence of set-off fails because the cross-claim relied upon was not yet due when notice of set-off was given.
12. In my judgment the claim succeeds in the amount of £3,256.37, being the agreed price less the sum allowed for the proved defects.
ORDER: (1) The Defendant shall pay the Claimant £3,256.37 with interest under section 69 of the County Courts Act 1984 from 3 July 2023 until payment.
(2) The Defendant shall pay the Claimant's costs of £503.09, of which £282.77 is for legal representation, and the unpaid court fee of £12.47 is to be recovered from the Defendant.
Permission to appeal must be sought within 21 days of this judgment, from this court or from the appeal court (CPR 52.12). This judgment is enforceable on expiry of 14 days.
