UNITED STATES COURT OF APPEALS FOR THE SEVENTH CIRCUIT
No. 22-7699
Arthur Wilson, Plaintiff-Appellee, v. Jeremy Flores, Defendant-Appellant.
OPINION
01/26/2023
Before Joan Ford, Frank Jones and Gary Ferguson, Circuit Judges. Appearances: Gloria Thomas for Appellant; Logan Nelson for Appellee.
Argued 01/12/2023; decided 01/26/2023. Defendant appeals from the judgment of the district court entered on 05/31/2022 awarding Plaintiff $13,174.22. Notice of appeal was filed on 06/20/2022.
Plaintiff Arthur Wilson seeks to recover amounts allegedly due under a written contract. Defendant Jeremy Flores denies liability, asserting partial performance and setoff.
Plaintiff's complaint, filed 05/19/2021, sought $19,244.54. After a bench trial the district court awarded $13,174.22 plus interest. Defendant argues on appeal that the court clearly erred in its findings of fact.
On 06/05/2020 the parties entered into a written contract under which Plaintiff undertook to perform certain work and Defendant undertook to pay the price within fourteen days of acceptance. The work was accepted without reservation.
The price was not paid by 04/22/2021. A letter requiring payment was sent on 04/27/2021. It went unanswered.
The district court credited the testimony of Lisa Simmons; credibility determinations of this kind are entitled to deference on appeal.
We review the district court's findings of fact for clear error and its conclusions of law de novo. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a)(6).
The principle that a party who accepts work without reservation cannot later resist payment on the basis of defects readily apparent on inspection is stated in Holmes v. Cruz, 701 F.4th 755 (2d Cir. 2022), where the court held that acceptance is strong evidence of conformity with the contract.
See also Griffith v. Brown, 50 F. Supp. 3d 995 (N.D. Ill. 2022) (holding that interest runs from the contractual due date without any further demand).
Compare Peterson v. Benson, 947 F.4th 1066 (5th Cir. 2022), and the authorities cited there on the assessment of damages for unpaid contract sums.
The setoff defense fails because the cross-claim on which Defendant relies had not matured when Defendant invoked it.
The judgment of the district court awarding $13,174.22 is AFFIRMED.
Costs on appeal are taxed against Defendant-Appellant under Fed. R. App. P. 39(a)(2).
ORDER: AFFIRMED. Appellant shall pay the costs of this appeal, fixed at $574.73.
A petition for rehearing may be filed within fourteen days after entry of judgment under Fed. R. App. P. 40; the mandate will issue seven days thereafter.
