UNITED STATES COURT OF APPEALS FOR THE FIRST CIRCUIT
No. 22-5797
Karen Davis, Plaintiff-Appellee, v. Michael Castillo, Defendant-Appellant.
OPINION
12/27/2022
Before Kathleen Morris, Doris McKinney and John Perez, Circuit Judges. Appearances: Jeremy Parks for Appellant; Donna Campbell for Appellee.
Argued 12/19/2022; decided 12/27/2022. Defendant appeals from the judgment of the district court entered on 07/08/2022 awarding Plaintiff $55,453.20. Notice of appeal was filed on 08/08/2022.
Plaintiff Karen Davis seeks to recover amounts allegedly due under a written contract. Defendant Michael Castillo denies liability, asserting partial performance and setoff.
Plaintiff's complaint, filed 02/16/2022, sought $78,402.84. After a bench trial the district court awarded $55,453.20 plus interest. Defendant argues on appeal that the court clearly erred in its findings of fact.
On 06/16/2021 the parties entered into a written contract under which Plaintiff undertook to perform certain work and Defendant undertook to pay the price within fourteen days of acceptance. The work was accepted without reservation.
The price was not paid by 12/27/2021. A letter requiring payment was sent on 01/25/2022. It went unanswered.
The district court credited the testimony of Jennifer Thomas; credibility determinations of this kind are entitled to deference on appeal.
We review the district court's findings of fact for clear error and its conclusions of law de novo. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a)(6).
The principle that a party who accepts work without reservation cannot later resist payment on the basis of defects readily apparent on inspection is stated in Garner v. Clark, 314 F. Supp. 3d 1056 (S.D.N.Y. 2021), where the court held that acceptance is strong evidence of conformity with the contract.
See also Williams v. Sanchez, 914 F.4th 169 (3d Cir. 2021) (holding that interest runs from the contractual due date without any further demand).
Compare Garcia v. Jones, 393 F. Supp. 3d 598 (N.D. Cal. 2022), and the authorities cited there on the assessment of damages for unpaid contract sums.
The setoff defense fails because the cross-claim on which Defendant relies had not matured when Defendant invoked it.
The judgment of the district court awarding $55,453.20 is AFFIRMED.
Costs on appeal are taxed against Defendant-Appellant under Fed. R. App. P. 39(a)(2).
ORDER: AFFIRMED. Appellant shall pay the costs of this appeal, fixed at $2,715.24.
A petition for rehearing may be filed within fourteen days after entry of judgment under Fed. R. App. P. 40; the mandate will issue seven days thereafter.
