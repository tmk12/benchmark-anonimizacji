IN THE COURT OF APPEAL (CIVIL DIVISION)
Case No. CA-2019-243452
ON APPEAL FROM THE COUNTY COURT. BETWEEN Barbara Graham (Appellant) and Scott Graham (Respondent)
JUDGMENT
29 January 2020
Before: Lady Justice Rodriguez (presiding), Lady Justice Moore and Lady Justice Johnson.
Counsel for the Appellant: Christine Pearson. Counsel for the Respondent: Joshua Alvarez.
Heard on 27 January 2020. Judgment given on 29 January 2020. This is an appeal from the judgment of the County Court of 30 May 2019, ordering the Appellant to pay the sum of £8,475.05. Notice of appeal was filed on 8 July 2019.
1. This is a claim by Scott Graham for the unpaid price of works carried out under a written agreement. Barbara Graham defends the claim on the grounds of part performance and set-off.
2. The Respondent's claim for £10,149.56 was issued on 24 December 2018. At trial the judge awarded £8,475.05 with interest and costs. The Appellant now argues that the judge misdirected himself on the evidence.
3. On 5 June 2018 the parties entered into a written contract under which the claimant undertook to carry out certain works and the defendant undertook to pay the price within fourteen days of acceptance. The works were accepted without reservation.
4. The price was not paid by 6 November 2018. A letter requiring payment was sent on 26 November 2018. It went unanswered.
5. The judge below preferred the evidence of Jennifer Hall; there is no basis on which this court would interfere with that assessment of credibility.
6. The principles governing an appeal on fact are well settled: an appellate court will not interfere with findings of primary fact unless satisfied that the judge was plainly wrong (CPR 52.21(3)).
7. The principle that a party who accepts work without reservation cannot later resist payment on the basis of defects readily apparent on inspection is stated in Brown v Clark [2018] UKSC 60, where the court held that acceptance is strong evidence of conformity with the contract.
8. See also Brown v Foster [2018] AC 539, in which it was decided that interest runs from the contractual due date without any further demand.
9. Compare Cox v Warren [2019] UKSC 19, and the authorities cited there on the assessment of damages for unpaid contract sums.
10. The defence of set-off fails because the cross-claim relied upon was not yet due when notice of set-off was given.
11. In my judgment the appeal should be dismissed. The judge's findings were open to him on the evidence and his award of £8,475.05 was correct.
12. I would dismiss the appeal; my Lords will wish to hear any submissions on costs.
ORDER: (1) The appeal is dismissed. (2) The Appellant shall pay the Respondent's costs of the appeal, summarily assessed at £203.21.
A second appeal requires the permission of the Court of Appeal and is limited to an important point of principle or practice (CPR 52.7).
